# Exclusion Zone — South Atlantic, 1982 · Quick Manual

Quick game guide — the same one shown by the **?** button (**H** key) during a game.

## Game

### GAME MODES

- **Two players:** whoever creates the room plays Argentina (Blue) and shares the 6-letter code; whoever joins with the code plays the United Kingdom (Red).
- **Solo vs. BOT:** pick Argentina or the United Kingdom on the home page. The opponent's doctrine (divided or concentrated formation, offensive or defensive posture) can be chosen or randomized.
- **Facilitated room:** the instructor opens the room and both players join with the code, choosing Blue or Red. The facilitator adjusts the forces before the start, creates and moves neutral units, authorizes movements and ratifies combat results. They can also send messages to one team or both.

### FORCES ON THE MAP

**Blue** = Argentina; **Red** = United Kingdom. **Neutral** units (green — merchant ships, fishing boats, hospital ship, research vessel, civil aircraft) are visible to everyone and cannot be attacked.

### CONTROLS

- **Click** one of your units to select it; click hexes to plot the route, or targets to attack.
- A hex with several units opens the stack list; **Group (together)** moves the whole stack.
- **Hover** over a unit to see its stats; **right-click** opens the full card.
- **Mouse wheel** or the + ⊙ − buttons zoom; drag with the middle button (or Alt + click) to pan the map.
- Keys: **H** help · **S** sound · **F** visual fog · **Esc** closes windows.

### CONNECTION AND LOG

If the connection drops or the page is reloaded, come back within **75 seconds** to resume the game where it was. **Export log** saves the game record; **Abandon game** ends the game and hands victory to the opponent.

The historical background of the exercise is in the **Case Study**, on the home page.

## Phases

### TURN STRUCTURE

Each **turn** is one day of operations with two periods: **☀ Day** and **🌙 Night**. Each period has a **Movement** phase and a **Combat** phase.

### SIMULTANEOUS MOVEMENT

Both sides plan at the same time. Select a unit and click, step by step, on the **green** hexes (the unit's range); **↩** undoes the last step. For ships, the range is colored by that period's fuel cost: green = 1 FP, amber = 2 FP and orange = 3 FP (**high speed**, from 3 hexes on). The enemy appears at its previous position until both sides confirm — finish with **End Movement**.

Ships sail only at sea; submarines cannot enter shallow water; land forces move only on land and along the coast. Embarked troops, special forces and aircraft that did not take off **follow the ship** carrying them; the landing force goes ashore once the ship reaches the islands' coast.

### MINEFIELDS

A ship or submarine entering an enemy-mined hex stops there and may take damage. Only **minesweepers** clear the mines and keep going.

### DETECTION AND NIGHT

You only see enemies within your units' detection range (fog of war). At night detection drops (−2), **except for submarines**, which use sonar. Submarines are only seen by units with anti-submarine detection range. Special forces are never detected; air bases and ports are always visible.

### WITH A FACILITATOR

Once both sides end movement, the facilitator **authorizes** the moves (and may deny or reposition a unit). After combat, they **ratify** the results before the turn advances. Meanwhile, the players wait.

### OPERATIONAL DEADLINE

The operation lasts at most **8 days**. When time runs out, whoever has made the most progress on their objectives wins.

## Combat

### DECLARING ATTACKS

In the combat phase, select a unit and click **red** targets (detected and in range). Choose the weapon and salvo size. Each registered engagement appears in a list on the panel and as a numbered arrow on the map — you can adjust the salvo or remove it before finishing with **End Engagement Phase**. Both sides' attacks are resolved interleaved, as if simultaneous.

### BATTLE ROUNDS

Each engagement has up to **2 rounds** (land-attack missiles have only one). After the first, both sides choose **CONTINUE** or **STOP**. Whoever continues alone gains the **initiative advantage** (rolls 2d6 and keeps the higher). In the second round the defending group **counter-attacks** with short-range weapons.

### INTERCEPTION AND GROUPS

Missiles and air attacks can be intercepted by the target's air defense — ships **stacked in the same hex** pool their defenses and defend as a group. Torpedoes and air-to-air missiles cannot be intercepted.

### DAMAGE AND DEGRADATION

Damage reduces **SP** (staying power); at 0 SP the unit is destroyed, taking with it any troops, aircraft and special forces on board. Non-lethal hits also **degrade** a random subsystem: detection, movement, combat capability or maximum fuel.

## Logistics

### FUEL POINTS (FP)

Ships and conventional submarines have limited FP. Cost per period: holding position = 1 FP, moving 1 hex = 1, 2 hexes = 2, 3 or more = 3 (at most 4 FP per period). Attacking and absorbing damage cost +1 FP each.

**0 FP = unit out of action**: it cannot move, attack or defend until refuelled.

### NAVAL REFUELLING

End the period **stacked** with a friendly tanker, logistics ship or port: FP return to maximum. Nuclear submarines do not use FP.

### AIRCRAFT

Aircraft have FP = 2× movement and only burn fuel in the air. After combat they return to base (air base or carrier) and are ready the next period with a full tank. An airborne aircraft that runs out of fuel away from a base is **lost**.

**Air-to-air refuelling:** a ready aircraft that starts the period in the same hex as a friendly tanker (KC-130 or Victor) gets **+50% FP** above its normal tank, extending that sortie's range.

### AMMUNITION

Missiles and torpedoes are **finite** (see N/N in the panel). Restocking: Blue ships and submarines that stay put in an Argentine port, aircraft returning to base, and Blue land units. The Red force **cannot restock naval weapons at sea** — save your salvos.

## Victory

### Blue Force — 3 OF 5 OBJECTIVES

- Destroy the Carrier
- Neutralize 2 of 3 Logistics Ships
- Neutralize the Amphibious Group
- Destroy the Nuclear Submarine
- Degrade ≥50% of Surface Combatants

### Red Force — 3 OF 3 OBJECTIVES

- Degrade ≥50% of Local Air/Naval Presence (Pucará, patrols, minesweepers)
- Degrade ≥40% of the Islands' Garrison
- Land a ground unit on the Islands

### DEADLINE

At the end of **8 days** with no winner, whoever has the greater progress toward their objectives wins — progress counts **partial damage**, not just completed conditions. The **VICTORY OBJECTIVES** panel tracks both sides in real time.

## Glossary

### TERMS AND ABBREVIATIONS

- **SP** — Staying Power — the unit's "hit points"
- **FP** — Fuel Points — the unit's endurance
- **MOV** — Movement — hexes per period
- **Det S/Air/Sub/L** — Detection range against Surface / Air / Submarine / Land
- **Period** — Half of a turn (☀ day or 🌙 night), with one movement and one combat phase
- **Stack** — Units in the same hex — they move together with “Group” and defend as a group
- **Neutral** — Civilian contact (green) created by the facilitator — visible to all, cannot be attacked

### WEAPONS AND CAPABILITIES

- **ASCM** — Anti-Ship Cruise Missile — long range against surface ships
- **MSS** — Surface-to-Surface Missile — short range against surface ships
- **TORPEDO** — Torpedo — against ships and submarines; cannot be intercepted
- **LACM** — Land-Attack Cruise Missile — long range against land targets
- **GUN** — Naval Gun — short range, does not consume magazine ammunition
- **AAD** — Air Defense — intercepts missiles and engages aircraft
- **ASW** — Anti-Submarine Warfare — detection and attack against submarines
- **AIR ATK** — Air Attack — aircraft against ships, aircraft, or land targets
- **SOF** — Special Forces Raid — attacks on installations and ships
- **AAM** — Air-to-Air Missile — fighter vs. fighter combat, cannot be intercepted
- **MINES** — Minefield — passive damage to whoever crosses the hex; only minesweepers can clear it
